Leverage in Trading: What a 1% Move Does to a Rs 20,000 Account

Updated October 2026 · Money Moves India 2 min read

Leverage lets you hold a trade that is bigger than the money in your account. It sounds like free money. It is not: it makes every gain and every loss bigger. This guide shows exactly how much bigger, using one simple account of Rs 20,000.

WordPlain meaning
PipThe smallest standard price step in forex. For most pairs it is 0.0001, for yen pairs 0.01.
LotThe size of a trade. One standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency. A mini lot is 10,000 and a micro lot is 1,000.
LeverageBorrowed buying power from the broker. 5x leverage means Rs 20,000 controls a Rs 1,00,000 position.

Pips and lots in one minute

A pip measures how far price moved. A lot says how much you are trading. Together they give the money result. On a pair quoted in US dollars, one pip is worth about $10 on a standard lot, about $1 on a mini lot and about $0.10 on a micro lot. A bigger lot means every pip is worth more, in both directions.

Leverage is borrowed buying power

Say you have Rs 20,000 in your account. With no leverage (1x) you can hold at most Rs 20,000 worth of an asset. With 5x leverage, the broker lets you hold Rs 1,00,000. You still lose or gain on the full Rs 1,00,000, not just on your Rs 20,000. The money you put in is called margin.

Worked example: a 1% price move

Now let the price move 1% against you. Look at what happens to the same Rs 20,000 at different leverage levels.

LeveragePosition sizeLoss from a 1% moveShare of your account
1xRs 20,000Rs 2001%
5xRs 1,00,000Rs 1,0005%
20xRs 4,00,000Rs 4,00020%
100xRs 20,00,000Rs 20,000100% (account gone)

The price move is the same in every row. Only the position size changed. At 100x, an ordinary 1% move can erase the whole account, and in practice the broker usually closes your trade even earlier (a stop-out). The same maths works for profits, but losses arrive faster than most beginners expect.

How to use leverage more safely

  • Decide your maximum loss first. Many beginners pick 1% to 2% of the account, which is Rs 200 to Rs 400 here.
  • Always set a stop-loss before you enter.
  • Work out position size from the stop: position size = maximum loss ÷ stop distance.

Example: maximum loss Rs 400 and a stop 1% away gives a position of Rs 40,000. That is only 2x leverage on Rs 20,000. Notice that the risk plan decided the leverage, not the other way round.
